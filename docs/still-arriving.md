# I already tried returning it. What next?

Start with what actually happened. Which sender keeps appearing? Is this another physical envelope or only an Informed Delivery preview? What did the household try, on what date, and is there a receipt or reply?

These questions came from people describing repeated attempts, not from a new guaranteed removal procedure. In a [January 2026 homeowner discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/homeowners/comments/1qpnhfo/mail_for_previous_owners/), people reported success after contacting individual senders. A [July 2025 technical discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44635867) included previews of former residents' mail that never physically arrived. Those are useful failure cases; anecdotes do not establish a success rate.

## Work out which problem remains

| Observation | Useful next step |
|---|---|
| The envelope has someone else's street address or apartment | Treat it as a delivery error, not an opt-out for your household. USPS says to return it to the carrier or mailbox without altering its information. |
| It is addressed only to someone confirmed to have moved out | USPS directs current occupants to mark "Not at this address," preserve the address and return it to the mailstream. Keep it unopened. |
| It says the old name **or Current Resident** | Occupant wording is a valid addressing format. A departed name does not make this a former-resident return. For advertising, investigate an address-based sender request. |
| The same advertiser keeps sending new pieces after returns | Check its current official correction/removal process. Unendorsed undeliverable Marketing Mail is disposed of by USPS; a returned catalog need not reach the sender or change its records. |
| A request was submitted but no result is clear | Inspect the original receipt, any required confirmation and the provider's stated processing window. Preserve the attempt; do not automatically submit another one. |
| A provider reports a change, then another piece arrives | Record the new observation and check the label, recipient, unit and provider scope. It could be another list or material already prepared. Ask for a reviewed follow-up where appropriate; don't declare the original request failed from the sender name alone. |
| Delivery errors continue across multiple senders | Prepare a short history for the verified local delivery office. Phone, email, letter and an in-person complaint are available; a paid or certified letter is not a required first step. |
| Only a preview appeared | Record it as a preview. Ask whether the household physically received it before describing continued delivery. |

Procedure sources checked October 20, 2026: [USPS misdelivery and Marketing Mail guidance](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/How-is-Undeliverable-and-Misdelivered-Mail-Handled), [USPS addressing, sections 3.1.3 and 3.4.1](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/602.htm), and [USPS complaints, section 6.1](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/608.htm). Recheck the relevant source before action.

## Use the pieces already in this kit

- **Different sender, different route.** Use [contact routes](routes.md) to verify email, form, phone or postal instructions. A broad opt-out is not evidence that every sender is covered.
- **Make the household clear.** The [two mailbox notes](household-kit.md) distinguish current names from confirmed former residents. They are informal aids, not official forms or guarantees.
- **Keep one history.** [Record outcomes](outcomes.md), including corrections and later observations. The next-step view can identify required confirmation or review; it does not send anything.
- **Choose review dates deliberately.** Use the provider's stated timing and the user's preference. A review date is not a promised resolution date.

Do not turn forum suggestions about discarding someone else's mail, changing their address, obscuring addresses or using internal postal codes into kit instructions. Unknown names need household confirmation. Important notices stay out of advertising-removal automation.

## Did it help?

If the household wants to evaluate reduction, keep a dated count of physically received pieces from the selected sender over clearly defined periods. Record days not checked and which recipient names were included. A few selected photos cannot establish a percentage reduction or prove a sender stopped mailing.

A manual comparison is an observation with limitations. It does not change the kit's `mail_reduction_verified` flag or establish causation. The most useful next report may simply be: "Request submitted on this date; sender acknowledged it; two further catalogs physically arrived."
